1. THE PROBLEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Commodore of the Coconut Grove Sailing Club needs a simple program to track the costs of the
boats that the club owns: sailing boats and coach (power) boats. Boats need maintenance, and to
control the expenditure the Commodore has a policy:

        The club may not spend more on a boat than it cost to buy the boat.

The program keeps the data of the fleet from one run to the next. It prints the boat inventory,
adds and removes boats, and gives or refuses permission each time money is to be spent on a boat.


2. WHAT THE PROGRAM MUST DO
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Information held about each boat
      - the type of boat: sailing or power
      - the name of the boat
      - the year of manufacture
      - the make/model
      - the length in feet (up to 100')
      - the purchase price (less than a million dollars)
      - the expenses maintaining the boat

2.2 The fleet
      - The club owns an arbitrary number of boats.
      - The boats are reported in the order in which they joined the fleet: the order of the lines
        of the CSV file, then the order in which boats were added.

2.3 Files
      - Initialising run: the fleet data is provided in a Comma Separated Values (CSV) text file,
        such as FleetData.csv, whose name is given as a command line parameter. Each line of the
        file describes one boat:
                type,name,year of manufacture,make/model,length,purchase price
        for example
                POWER,Big Brother,2019,Mako,20,12989.56
        The file holds no expenses: nothing has been spent yet on a boat that comes from it.
      - Upon exiting, the program saves the fleet data in serialized form in a file called
        FleetData.db.
      - Subsequent ("normal") runs: no command line parameter is given, and the fleet data is read
        from FleetData.db instead.

2.4 Menu
    Once the fleet data is loaded the program offers a menu, again and again until the user exits:
      (P)rint     print the boat inventory
      (A)dd       add a boat
      (R)emove    remove a boat
      (E)xpense   request permission to spend money on a boat, subject to the Commodore's policy
      e(X)it      exit the program

2.5 The Commodore's policy
      - A request to spend an amount on a boat is authorized if the money already spent on the boat
        plus the amount is not more than the purchase price of the boat. The amount is then added
        to the expenses of the boat, and the program reports the total spent on the boat so far.
      - Otherwise the request is not permitted: nothing is spent, and the program reports how much
        is left to spend on the boat (its purchase price less its expenses).
      - Example from the assignment: Moon Glow was bought for $5500.00.
                spend 3456.78    authorized       "$3456.78 spent"            3456.78 <= 5500.00
                spend 2000       authorized       "$5456.78 spent"            5456.78 <= 5500.00
                spend 250        not permitted    "only $43.22 left to spend" 5706.78 >  5500.00

2.6 User friendliness, and what may be assumed
      - Menu options are accepted in upper and lower case.
      - Boat names are accepted without regard for case.
      - An option that is not in the menu is reported ("Invalid menu option, try again") and the
        menu is offered again.
      - When the boat to remove, or to spend on, is not in the fleet, this is reported
        ("Cannot find boat" and the name) and the program goes back to the menu.
      - A boat is added by typing a string that looks like one line of the CSV file.
      - The boat data in the CSV file is correctly formatted, within the limits given in 2.1.
      - Numeric input is syntactically correct.

2.7 The boat inventory (fleet report)
    One line for each boat with its type, name, year of manufacture, make/model, length, the price
    paid for it and the money spent on it, then one line with the totals paid and spent for the
    whole fleet.


3. FINDING THE OBJECTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The nouns of the problem are the candidates for the classes and for their attributes.

  Noun in the problem                     What it becomes
  --------------------------------------  ----------------------------------------------------------
  Commodore, club                         Outside the program: the people who use it and who own
                                          the boats. No class.
  program (Fleet Management System)       The application: the main class FleetManagement. It talks
                                          to the user (menu, keyboard, screen) and handles the
                                          files.
  boat (sailing boat, power boat)         Class Boat: the main object of the problem.
  type of boat (sailing, power)           Attribute of a Boat. There are only two fixed values, so
                                          it is an enumerated type, BoatType - not two subclasses
                                          of Boat, because sailing boats and power boats hold the
                                          same information and are treated in the same way.
  name, year of manufacture, make/model,  Attributes of a Boat.
  length, purchase price, expenses
  costs, money, expenditure, maintenance  Not objects of their own: they are the purchase price
                                          and the expenses of a Boat.
  policy (the Commodore's policy)         A rule about the money of one boat: a behaviour of Boat,
                                          which knows its purchase price and its expenses.
  fleet, boat inventory                   Class Fleet: the boats that the club owns, any number of
                                          them.
  fleet data, CSV file, FleetData.db,     The fleet as it is stored outside the program. Reading
  command line parameter                  and writing the files are behaviours of FleetManagement.
  menu, option, user, keyboard input      The user interface: behaviours of FleetManagement.
  fleet report, total                     What the Fleet tells about itself, made of what each
                                          Boat tells about itself.

Classes found:   FleetManagement,   Fleet,   Boat,   BoatType.


4. WHAT EACH OBJECT KNOWS AND DOES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The verbs of the problem (track, hold, spend, provide, save, read, print, add, remove, request,
exit) give the behaviours. Each behaviour is given to the object that knows the information that
the behaviour needs.

4.1 Boat - one boat of the club
    Knows (attributes)
      - its type (a BoatType), name, year of manufacture, make/model, length in feet and purchase
        price, which never change once the boat exists
      - its expenses: the money spent so far maintaining it, nothing for a new boat
    Does (behaviours)
      - is created from its type, name, year of manufacture, make/model, length and purchase price
      - tells whether it has a given name, without regard for case
      - tells its purchase price, and its expenses
      - tells how much may still be spent on it
      - answers a request to spend an amount: it applies the Commodore's policy and, if the
        expense is authorized, adds the amount to its expenses
      - describes itself as one line of the fleet report

4.2 Fleet - the boats of the club
    Knows (attributes)
      - its boats: any number of Boat objects, in the order in which they joined the fleet
    Does (behaviours)
      - starts with no boats
      - adds a boat
      - finds a boat by its name, without regard for case
      - removes a boat by its name, without regard for case
      - totals the purchase prices, and the expenses, of its boats
      - describes itself as the fleet report: one line for each boat, then the totals

4.3 BoatType - the type of a boat
    One of two values: SAILING or POWER.

4.4 FleetManagement - the program
    Knows
      - nothing that changes: only fixed values, such as the name of the file FleetData.db and
        the letters of the menu options. The boats belong to the Fleet object.
    Does (behaviours)
      - displays the welcome message and the exit message
      - loads the fleet from the CSV file named on the command line (initialising run), or from
        FleetData.db (normal run)
      - makes a Boat from one line of CSV data, which comes from the CSV file or from the keyboard
      - displays the menu, and reads and checks the option of the user
      - carries out the options by asking the Fleet and its Boats: print, add, remove, expense
      - saves the fleet in FleetData.db when the user exits
    It is the only class that reads the keyboard, writes on the screen and handles the files.


5. HOW THE CLASSES ARE RELATED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      +-----------------+   uses    +-------+  has 0..*   +------+   has 1    +----------+
      | FleetManagement |- - - - - >| Fleet |<>---------->| Boat |----------->| BoatType |
      +-----------------+           +-------+             +------+            +----------+
               |                                              ^
               + - - - - - - - - - - creates - - - - - - - - -+

  - FleetManagement uses one Fleet: it loads it, hands it to the menu options, and saves it.
  - A Fleet HAS boats: any number of Boat objects (has-a).
  - A Boat HAS a type: one BoatType value (has-a).
  - FleetManagement creates the Boat objects from CSV data, and gives them to the Fleet.
  - No inheritance (is-a) is needed: there is only one kind of Boat (see 3), and nothing else in
    the problem is a special case of something more general.
  - A Fleet is saved in FleetData.db with all its Boats, and is read back whole.


6. HOW THE OBJECTS WORK TOGETHER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Start of an initialising run (the command line names the CSV file)
      1. FleetManagement displays the welcome message.
      2. FleetManagement opens the CSV file and creates an empty Fleet.
      3. For each line of the file, FleetManagement makes a Boat from the line and asks the Fleet
         to add it.
      4. FleetManagement closes the file and offers the menu.

6.2 Start of a normal run (no command line parameter)
      1. FleetManagement displays the welcome message.
      2. FleetManagement reads the Fleet, with its Boats and their expenses, from FleetData.db,
         closes the file and offers the menu.

6.3 Print
      1. FleetManagement asks the Fleet for its report.
      2. The Fleet asks each Boat for its line of the report, and adds the line of the totals.
      3. FleetManagement displays the report.

6.4 Add
      1. FleetManagement asks the user for the CSV data of the new boat.
      2. FleetManagement makes a Boat from the data and asks the Fleet to add it.

6.5 Remove
      1. FleetManagement asks the user for the name of the boat.
      2. FleetManagement asks the Fleet to remove the boat that has this name. The Fleet asks its
         Boats whether they have the name, and removes the one that does.
      3. If the Fleet has no such boat, FleetManagement displays "Cannot find boat" and the name.

6.6 Expense
      1. FleetManagement asks the user for the name of the boat.
      2. FleetManagement asks the Fleet to find the boat that has this name. If the Fleet has no
         such boat, FleetManagement displays "Cannot find boat" and the name, and the option ends.
      3. FleetManagement asks the user for the amount.
      4. FleetManagement asks the Boat to spend the amount. The Boat applies the Commodore's
         policy.
      5. Authorized: the Boat has added the amount to its expenses, and FleetManagement displays
         the total spent on the boat.
         Not permitted: the Boat is unchanged, and FleetManagement displays how much is left to
         spend on the boat.

6.7 Exit
      1. FleetManagement saves the Fleet, with its Boats and their expenses, in FleetData.db and
         closes the file.
      2. FleetManagement displays the exit message.


7. CHECK: EVERY REQUIREMENT HAS AN OBJECT RESPONSIBLE FOR IT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Requirement                                               Responsible
  --------------------------------------------------------  ----------------------------------------
  Hold the information about each boat (2.1)                Boat, BoatType
  Arbitrary number of boats (2.2)                           Fleet
  Read the CSV file named on the command line (2.3)         FleetManagement
  Save the fleet in FleetData.db, and read it back (2.3)    FleetManagement
  Menu, options in upper and lower case (2.4, 2.6)          FleetManagement
  Print the boat inventory (2.4, 2.7)                       Fleet and Boat make the report,
                                                            FleetManagement displays it
  Add a boat typed as CSV data (2.4, 2.6)                   FleetManagement makes the Boat,
                                                            Fleet adds it
  Remove a boat, name without regard for case (2.4, 2.6)    Fleet (each Boat compares the names)
  Request to spend, the Commodore's policy (2.4, 2.5)       Boat (found by the Fleet)
  Invalid menu option, boat not found (2.6)                 FleetManagement displays the messages
